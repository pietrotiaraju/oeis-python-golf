c6f75beabf62 Lucas-Lehmer-Riesel test with P=4 (Riesel 1969; Rodseth 1994) on N=c*2^n-1 for c=7,11,13,17,19 (3 does not divide c). The skipped parity of n makes 3 divide N, so N is composite. For the tried parity N=7 mod 8 and N=1 mod 3, so (2|N)=1 and (3|N)=-1, and once 2^n>c the theorem says N is prime iff N divides u_{n-2}, where u_0=V_c(4) is the hard-coded seed and u_{i+1}=u_i^2-2 (computed as s*s%N-2, whose only multiple of N in -2..N-3 is 0). The first term is printed by s=0 before any test runs; the other n with 2^n<c (c=17 n=4, c=19 n=3) give primes that the test passes, checked directly. Exact integers, so correct for every n; the bars are small only because the next terms are in the thousands (each page also proves the 'passing N is prime' direction).
