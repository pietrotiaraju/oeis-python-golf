Odd composite k are skipped through Wilson's theorem (P = (k-1)!, P%k = 0 exactly for composite k), which is right because (10^q+1)/11 properly divides N for any odd prime q | k, q < k. For prime k every prime factor p of N other than 11 has ord_p(10) = 2k (order 2 would force p | 99), so p = 1 (mod 2k); and 11 | N only when 11 | k, i.e. k = 11, whose N has the factor 23 = 2*11+1. So trial division over d = 1 (mod 2k) up to math.isqrt(N) is a complete primality proof for every k; it rests only on Wilson's and Fermat's little theorems.
