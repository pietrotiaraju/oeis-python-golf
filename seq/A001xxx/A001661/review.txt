1fca04a57357 Definition: a(n) is the largest number that is not a sum of distinct positive n-th powers (exists by Sprague 1948). S is the bitset of subset sums of 1^n..(k-1)^n cut to X bits (exact below X, since powers are positive; X doubles and the run restarts from S=1 whenever the window would not fit). The loop stops at the first k whose window [k^n, 2k^n) is all set and prints the largest clear bit m < k^n. The page proves: (1) a full window forces 2k^n >= (k+1)^n (else 1^n+..+(k-1)^n < k^n), hence 2j^n >= (j+1)^n for all j >= k; (2) by induction the interval [P, P+L) of sums grows by j^n at each later power, so every m >= P is a sum (Richert-type lemma); (3) numbers below k^n use only smaller powers, so the clear bits below P are exactly the non-sums there, and the printed m is a(n); (4) termination: for large k, (k-1)^n-(k-2)^n > a(n) and 2k^n < (k-1)^n+2(k-2)^n, and then every m in the window is a sum of distinct powers below k^n; (5) restarts end once X >= 2k1^n. Exact integers, so the output is a(n) for every n given memory; n=6 starts with X=2^40 bits and stops with MemoryError after the bar. 4 terms fit in a table shorter than the program, hence the review.
